💡 How it works

A whole clause in a noun’s slot

A noun clause = connector + subject + verb, doing a noun’s job. The connectors are that · what · whatever · when · where · why · how · whether · if · who · whoever · whom · whose · which.     💡 The golden rule

    One sentence, one act of asking

    Inversion is the question signal itself. An embedded clause only reports the content of a question, so it never inverts and never takes do/does/did. Pick a question, read the inverted copy, then repair it. (Dentro de la cláusula: orden de enunciado, sin do/does.)

    Direct question — inversion allowed
    ✘ Question order copied into the clause
    ✔ Statement order
    ❓ The sentence ends with ? only if the main clause asks: Do you know where the file is? — but still where the file is inside.
    ✨ The subjunctive

    The base form after demand, insist, recommend, suggest, require

    After these verbs — and after it is essential / vital / important that — the that-clause takes the base form: no -s, no tense, and be stays be. (Subjuntivo: forma base, aunque el sujeto sea de tercera persona.)

    💡 C1 refinement

    Extraposition: It is clear that… vs That … is clear

    A clause-subject is correct but front-heavy. English prefers light before heavy: a dummy it takes the subject seat and the clause moves to the end. Some verbs accept only that version. (Sujeto ligero primero, cláusula pesada al final.)

    Extraposed — dummy it in front
    ⚠️ Extraposition-only verbs: appear, turn out, seem, happen — ✘ That he lied appears. ✔ It appears that he lied.

    🧠 Why English works this way

    Inverting twice would mean asking twice

    Subject–auxiliary inversion is not decoration: it is how English marks “I am asking.” An embedded clause merely reports the content of a question — nobody is asking inside it. One sentence, one act: if the main clause asks, it inverts and it takes the question mark; the clause inside does neither. That is why I wonder where the file is resists inversion. (Una oración, un acto de preguntar.)

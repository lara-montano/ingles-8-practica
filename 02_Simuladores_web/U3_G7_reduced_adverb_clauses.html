💡 How it works

Cutting an adverb clause down to a phrase

An adverb clause reduces only when both clauses share the same subject. Delete the subject; convert the verb. -ing = active · -ed = passive · having + participle = earlier action. Pick a sentence and watch the cut. (Solo se reduce si el sujeto es el mismo en las dos cláusulas.)

    🔍 The detector — who does the -ing?

    Find the dangling participle

    Say which noun the writer meant the phrase to describe — the doer for an -ing phrase, the receiver for an -ed one.     ⭐ The absolute construction

    The one reduction that keeps its own subject

    Formal, compact and immune to dangling: the subject travels with the phrase, so it escapes the same-subject rule entirely. Recognize it on the exam; deploy it in reports. (Lleva su propio sujeto, por eso nunca queda colgado.)

    ⭐ Exceptions & traps

    Frozen formulas, having, being, and the connector that vanishes

    🧠 Why English works this way

    The participle pawned its subject

    That is the entire reason reductions require shared subjects — and the entire mechanism of the dangler. In Having finished the report, the printer jammed, the rule hands having finished to the printer, and the sentence claims your printer writes reports. The grammar did not fail; it worked perfectly on the wrong noun. Before reducing, always ask: who will the reader's rule pick? (La gramática no falló: se aplicó al sustantivo equivocado.)

    🚫 Traps for Spanish speakers

    Three ways this goes wrong

    • The dangler. Spanish tolerates a loose gerund clause; English does not. Having finished the report, the printer jammed. → Having finished the report, I found the printer jammed / After I finished the report, the printer jammed.
    • Reducing when the subjects differ. While the kiln was cooling, the apprentices while cooling cleaned the molds. → if the subjects differ, keep the full clause.
    • Active form for a passive meaning. When heating, aluminum melts — aluminum does not heat itself. → When heated, aluminum melts. Same trap in Once calibrating → Once calibrated and While transporting → While being transported.

    (El español admite gerundios sueltos; el inglés los entrega mecánicamente al sujeto de la principal.)

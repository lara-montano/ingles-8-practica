💡 How it works

Two equals, one bolt: the coordinate joint

A coordinate connector joins two independent clauses — each has its own subject and verb, and each could stand alone. The pattern: independent clause , + connector + independent clause. The comma appears before the connector, never after it. Pick a connector and watch the joint. (Une dos cláusulas independientes; la coma va antes del conector, nunca después.)

2 · Same weight? — the two kinds of joint

    additioncontrastalternativenegative additionreason (formal)result
    the tank drainedmain clause — stands alone ✔
    because the pump failedcannot stand alone ✘
    ⇒ The tank drained because the pump failed.

    The listener test. Say “because the pump failed” on its own and a listener waits for the rest. Say “the tank drained” and nobody waits. That is the whole difference between the two joints — and the reason a subordinate clause can never be punctuated as a sentence. A coordinate connector needs two equals: two clauses that both pass the test. (Coordinación = dos iguales; subordinación = una cláusula cuelga de la otra.)

    🔍 What the exam actually checks

    Inspect the joint: three checks

    When you see a coordinate connector in an exam sentence, run three checks: one subject–verb pair on each side · no comma splice · no double connector. Subjects are underlined in steel, verbs in orange. (Sujeto y verbo a cada lado; sin coma suelta; un solo conector.)

      Inversion, for, short clauses, so that, shared subject

      ⚠ Common mistakes — don’t fall in!

      Traps for Spanish speakers

      • however / therefore / moreover are NOT coordinate connectors. They are adverbs and cannot join clauses with just a comma — one of the most-tested traps at this level.
        ✘ The budget was cut, however the project survived.
        ✔ The budget was cut; however, the project survived. · ✔ The budget was cut, but the project survived.
      • Spanish pero tolerates starting after a comma anywhere; English but needs a full clause on each side inside one sentence. (“…, pero” suelto no es una oración en inglés.)
      • ✘ The results were clear, so that we published them. — so (result) does not take that. ✔ …, so we published them.
      🧬 C1 refinement — ellipsis

      The second mention can vanish

      Coordination does not just join clauses; it licenses deletion. Whatever the two sides share may be said once. The safety rule: the deleted words must be recoverable exactly — if restoring them forces a change, the ellipsis is illegal. (Lo compartido se dice una vez, pero debe poder restaurarse sin cambiar nada.)

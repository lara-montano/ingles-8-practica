💡 How it works

A clause folded into a noun phrase

An adjective (relative) clause follows a noun and describes it. Choose the connector by what it refers to and by the job it does inside its clause. The exam asks the second question again and again: is the connector also the subject? (¿El conector es además el sujeto de su cláusula?)

🔍 The count check

Count the subjects and verbs

Every clause needs exactly one subject for its verb. Count them and the two classic failures appear. (Un sujeto por verbo, ni más ni menos.)

🎛 The connector dial

Which connector, and why

Tap a connector to see its referent, its job, and whether it may be dropped or follow a comma. (Referente + función en la cláusula.)

, , The comma switch

Defining vs. non-defining at C1

Defining (no commas) picks out which one. Non-defining (commas) adds a side note. After a comma: never that, never omit the connector. (Las comas cambian el significado, no la puntuación.)

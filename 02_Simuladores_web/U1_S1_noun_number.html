📦 Academic uncountables

No a/an, no plural, singular verb

These nouns are uncountable in English even though Spanish pluralises them. Tap one to see its verb and how to count it. (Para contarlos se usa un partitivo: a piece of…)

🏛️ Irregular plurals of academic English

Latin and Greek endings

Tap Hide the plurals and test yourself: tap any cell to reveal it. (-is → -es · -on/-um → -a · -ix/-ex → -ices · -us → -i)

singular → plural
On formal exams treat data, criteria, phenomena, media as plurals (the data show). Everyday professional English also says the data shows — pick one register and stay in it. series and species do not change; index and formula accept two plurals (indices/indexes, formulas/formulae).
🧮 The quantifier grid

Counting vs. measuring — sort every quantifier

Every quantifier belongs to one column, and mixing columns is the error the exam plants. Tap a quantifier, then tap the column it belongs to. (Toca la palabra y después la columna; no hay que arrastrar.)

Countable — how many?

trials, items, parts, applicants

Uncountable — how much?

Two subtleties. few = almost none (bad news) vs. a few = some (mild good news); the same contrast holds for little / a little. And supermarket English (less items) is counted wrong on the exam: ✔ fewer items.
📊 Collectives, percentages, fractions · or / nor

Which noun gives the order?

After a percentage or a fraction, the verb agrees with the noun after of. With or and nor, the nearest element wins. Tap a case. (El sustantivo tras of manda; con or/nor manda el más cercano.)

💡 How it works

Two slots, one verdict

An English adjective normally has two homes: before the noun (attributive) and after a linking verb (predicative). Some words are locked out of one of them. Tap a word and the detector lights the slot it fits and crosses the one it cannot enter, with the reason. (Cada palabra se prueba en dos posiciones: antes del sustantivo y después del verbo.)

🎭 Disguise 1

-ly words that are adjectives, not adverbs

Not everything in -ly is an adverb. These eleven are adjectives, and English builds no manner adverb on top of them (✘ friendlily, ✘ costily), so for the adverb meaning you paraphrase. (Son adjetivos; para el adverbio hay que parafrasear.)

friendly · lovely · lonely · likely · lively · elderly · costly · deadly · silly · orderly · cowardly

✔ a costly mistake · The failure is likely. — To get the adverb meaning, paraphrase: in a friendly way / in a friendly manner.

The trap

He behaved friendly. → in a friendly way
She spoke very friendly. → in a very friendly way
He behaved cowardly. → in a cowardly way
costily / sillily / fastly → not standard English

Double agents — adjective and adverb

daily · weekly · monthly · yearly · early · fast · hard · late

a daily report (adjective) · reported daily (adverb). The deadline is close, so work fast.

🚫 Disguise 2

The a- family: after the verb only

These eight appear only after a verb, never before a noun. When you need the attributive position, swap in the equivalent. (Predicativos: van después del verbo; antes del sustantivo se usa el equivalente.)

afraid · alike · alive · alone · asleep · awake · ashamed · aware

Swap it for the attributive equivalent

the asleep operator → the sleeping operator
an alive specimen → a living specimen
the afraid intern → the frightened intern
an ashamed performance → a shameful performance
the alone technician → the solitary technician
the aware employees → the employees who were aware

The reverse club — attributive-only

Mirror image: these appear only before the noun and never after a verb — the main reason, a mere formality, an utter failure, the former director, the principal cause. ✘ The report was utter.

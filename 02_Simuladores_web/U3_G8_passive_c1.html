💡 Form: be + past participle, in every tense

Service every tense: the auxiliary chain

Every passive has two parts: an auxiliary chain that carries the tense (is · has been · will have been · should have been) and a past participle that never changes. Tap a tense and watch only the chain move. (Solo cambia la cadena de auxiliares; el participio queda fijo.)

⚠️ Pitfalls — the errors the exam plants

Typical errors of Spanish speakers

Each pair: the form the exam underlines → the repair. (Los errores que el examen siembra una y otra vez.)

✘ The samples have analyzed.✔ The samples have been analyzed.missing been
✘ must be replace✔ must be replacedbase form instead of participle after a modal
✘ The accident was occurred at dawn.✔ The accident occurred at dawn.intransitive verbs have no passive
✘ She born in 1998. · ✘ She was borned…✔ She was born in 1998.born is always passive — never without be
✘ Titanium is resembled by this alloy.✔ This alloy resembles titanium.state verbs refuse the passive
✘ announced with the dean✔ announced by the deanby = doer · with = instrument
✘ The seals need replaced.✔ The seals need replacing / need to be replaced.need + -ing (or to be + participle), never the hybrid
✘ have being verified✔ have been verifiedbeen follows have; being follows be (is being verified)
✘ The results was published.✔ The results were published.be agrees with the new subject
⚖️ Middle voice or passive?

A decision about causality

Some verbs describe a change with no doer at all: The door opened · The crack widened. The middle voice presents the event as natural change; the passive presents it as someone’s action with the doer offstage. Pick an event, read the cause, and tap the version a lab report needs. (Elegir entre voz media y pasiva es afirmar algo sobre la causa.)

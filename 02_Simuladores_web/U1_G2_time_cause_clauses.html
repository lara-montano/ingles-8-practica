💡 How it works

The subordinate joint

An adverb clause is a full clause (subject + verb) that modifies the main clause. A subordinating connector hangs it from the beam. Pick the connector, then slide the clause to the front or to the end and watch the comma and the tense react. (La cláusula adverbial cuelga de la principal; el conector es la junta.)

🧠 Why English works this way

No will inside a time clause

The time clause is not a prediction — it only names the trigger event. The main clause carries the future for the whole sentence, so marking the future twice would be redundant, and English deletes the redundancy.

✔ We will start when the client arrives.
✘ … when the client will arrive.
✔ Call me after you have finished.
✘ … after you will have finished.
Spanish does exactly the same thing with a different tool: you say cuando llegue (subjunctive), never cuando llegará. Same logic, different uniform — if you would use the subjunctive after cuando, use the present after when. (Mismo mecanismo, otra herramienta: subjuntivo en español, presente en inglés.)

The rule covers the whole family: until, before, after, once, as soon as, as long as, by the time.

🚨 Where Spanish speakers lose the point

The five traps of Grammar 2

  • 1 · during vs while. During is a preposition (+ noun); while takes a clause. ✔ during the test · ✔ while the test was running · ✘ during the test was running
  • 2 · because vs because of. Clause vs noun. ✔ because the pump failed · ✔ because of the pump failure · ✘ because of the pump failed
  • 3 · An adverb clause is not a sentence. ✘ Because the results were inconclusive. (fragment) — the exam loves to offer fragments as answer options.
  • 4 · The cuando reflex. Spanish uses a subjunctive after cuando and learners translate it as a future. English forbids it: ✘ when he will arrive · ✔ when he arrives.
  • 5 · The hasta que no reflex. Same family as trap 4: Spanish inserts a negative that English does not carry. ✘ until he does not come · ✔ until he comes. (Calco de «hasta que no llegue».)

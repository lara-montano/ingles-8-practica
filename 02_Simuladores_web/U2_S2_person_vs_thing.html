💡 How it works

One root, three roles

From one root, English builds a doer, a receiver and a thing or process. -er / -or / -ant = the one who does · -ee = the one it is done to · -tion / -ment / -ance = the act or the thing. Feed the machine a verb and read the endings. (Un mismo verbo da tres palabras; el sufijo decide el papel.)

⚙️ The -tion / -ment family

The act, or the thing it produces?

The same ending names either the act (what happens) or the result (what you end up with). The sentence tells you which. (Proceso o resultado: lo decide la oración.)

    👩‍🏫 For the teacher — how this page maps to the manual
    • Explore is Structure 2's three-way split: the role machine runs the manual's eleven roots (employ, train, interview, pay, address, license, invent, supervise, consult, apply, inspect) and colour-codes doer (-er/-or/-ant), receiver (-ee) and act/thing (-tion/-ment). The two figures fix employer vs employee; the sorter is a tap-to-select classifier (person · machine · act · field); the act/result card splits process from product; the exceptions card is the manual's exception box (machine -er, attendee/escapee/refugee, -ist vs -ian, engineer/engine) plus the pitfall box ("decide by slot, never by sound").
    • Build is productive: students assemble the word by naming the role and then choosing the family member (manual exercise A and Task set 8), then transform the manual's cloze paragraph and the homework contexts, with validation.
    • Drill merges the quizzes into one graded engine in the two exam formats (★ pick the family member · ★★ machine vs person, doer vs receiver · ★★★ act vs result, engineering/engineer, employer/employee) with a score and streak.
    • Works offline. Reading/writing only — no audio.
